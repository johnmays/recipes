# Vegetarian Mapo Tofu (as authentic as a white guy can get)
A nice Sichuan dish.  Often made with meat but you can easily ignore it or replace it with minced mushroom like I do.

## A important note about tofu:
According to [J. Kenji Lopez](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dElALuJ9Xo8&ab_channel=J.KenjiL%C3%B3pez-Alt), the underlying reality of tofu texture is that there are two independent qualities of a block of tofu that are often confused with eachother.  First, there are two popular coagulents used to make tofu, resulting in a variety that is most often labelled "silken" in English, and another variety that is very often left unlabeled in the states but that some call "cottony." Cottony is what, for example, Kroger brand tofu is.  It's more popular in the states.  On the other hand, silken is less popular.  The meaningful difference is that silken coagulent produces a smoother tofu.  Silken tofu doesn't really have the "meaty" quality cottony tofu can, and is much closer to a firmed-up pudding (that's the only thing I can think to compare it to).  It is also worth noting that silken is *somewhat* softer and falls apart much more easily.  The second axis is firmness, described by the labels "firm", "soft", "extra firm", "medium", etc, which are different than coagulent type from the. There are a lot of misnomers in American supermarkets implying that silken is just softer than soft made with the coagulent used to make the normal "cottony" varieties, which adds to the confusion.  For this dish, it seems like most chefs use either soft cottony, or extra firm/firm silken.  That's what I choose here.  You can't find those everywhere.  But I believe soft silken would be too soft and firm/extra firm cottony would be too tough.  I can't really speak to what is most often used in China, just what is most often used on the internet.
## Ingredients:
- one 250-350g pack of extra firm/firm silken tofu
- dried mushrooms (for the broth and the mince)
- kombu (optional)
- 1/2 tbsp of sichuan peppercorns
- 3 garlic cloves
- asian (red) chili powder
- A few tbsp of cooking oil
- 1 tsp "shaoxin" or "shao xing" cooking rice wine (usually comes with a red label)
- 1 tsp sugar
- 1 tsp corn starch
- 1 tsp sesame oil
- 1 tsp light soy sauce
- chili bean paste ("doubanjiang" or "toban djan") (usually also comes with a red label)
### Stuff I left out that is pretty common to include:
- 1 tsp dark chinese vinegar (goes in right after slurry)
- minced ginger (treat exactly like garlic)
## Instructions:
If you don't have a wok (I don't), cook this in a cast-iron pan or high-walled steel pan if you must.
You can do these four things simultaneously.
### Prep:
- **Rice:** 
Get some nice medium grain white rice going to eat this with.
- **Slurry:** 
In a small container, mix a tbsp of water with your tsp of corn starch and set aside.
- **Peppercorns:** 
Toast the peppercorn on medium-low heat until you can smell it and they leave oily little blotches on the pan, remove from heat.  Mash the shit out of them with a spice grinder or mortar, get them extremely fine.  You do not want crunchy peppercorn in your dish.  Set aside.
- **Broth:** 
Set a chunk of kombu and some dried mushrooms to boil with 1.25 cup of water.  Let them go at a simmer for about 15 minutes.  Add a tsp of msg if you're feeling salty.
### Main:
- Mince up a handful of the mushrooms from the broth, put them in a cold pan with the oil, and turn heat to medium-low.  Fry mushrooms for about 3 minutes.
- Add the curry bean paste to the oil
