# Vegetarian Mapo Tofu (J. Kenji Lopez Version)
An approximately Japanese version of a nice Sichuan dish.  Often made with meat, but you can do minced mushroom or impossible meat (this should be a once-per-month AT MOST ingredient).  This is almost completely from this J. Kenji Lopez video.  I think this dish is a nice way to switch it up, and takes way less time to prep than the Sichuan version.
## Sources:
[J. Kenji Lopez Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dElALuJ9Xo8&ab_channel=J.KenjiL%C3%B3pez-Alt)

## A important note about tofu:
According to Lopez (in the video above), the underlying reality of tofu texture is that there are two independent qualities of a block of tofu that are often confused with each other.  First, there are two popular coagulents used to make tofu, resulting in a variety that is most often labelled "silken" in English, and another variety that is very often left unlabeled in the states but that some call "cottony." Cottony is what, for example, Kroger brand tofu is.  It's more popular in the states.  On the other hand, silken is less popular.  The meaningful difference is that silken coagulent produces a smoother tofu.  Silken tofu doesn't really have the "meaty" quality cottony tofu can, and is much closer to a firmed-up pudding (that's the only thing I can think to compare it to).  It is also worth noting that silken is *somewhat* softer and falls apart much more easily.  The second axis is firmness, described by the labels "firm", "soft", "extra firm", "medium", etc, which is completely separate from coagulent type. There are a lot of misnomers in American supermarkets implying that silken is just softer than soft made with the coagulent used to make the normal "cottony" varieties, which adds to the confusion.  For this dish, it seems like most chefs use either soft cottony, or extra firm/firm silken.  The latter is what I choose here.  You can't find those everywhere.  But I believe soft silken would be too soft and firm/extra firm cottony would be too tough.  I can't really speak to what is most often used in Asia, just what is most often used on the internet.

## Ingredients:
- 2 tbsp of cooking oil
- one 250-350g pack of extra firm/firm silken tofu
- 3 garlic cloves
- knob of ginger similar in volume to your three garlic cloves
- 2 stalk of green onion
- A few tbsp of cooking oil
- 2 tbsp sake ("shaoxin" or "shao xing" Chinese cooking rice wine is a hardly noticeable substitute)
- 2 tbsp of mirin
- 2.5 tbsp shoyu (or just dark soy sauce, like Kikkoman)
- 3 tsp corn starch
### Optional: 
- 1 tsp sesame oil
## Instructions:
If you don't have a wok (I don't), cook this in a cast-iron pan or high-walled steel pan if you must.
### Prep:
- **Make chili oil or chili crisp if you don't have it**
- **Chop up your garlic, onions, and ginger:**
Mince the garlic and ginger quite fine.  Set all aside together.
- **Rice:** 
Get some nice medium grain white rice going to eat this with.
- **Tofu:**
Chop up your tofu into cubes that you think are appropriate.  I would do 1/2" to 3/4".
### Main:
- Put the two tbsp of cooking oil in the pan with the "meat"
- Put the heat on medium to heat everything up
- Cook until "meat" is browned or begins sticking to the pan
- Add the ginger, garlic, and onions from earlier
- Let go for a few minutes, pushing everything around
- Add soy sauce, wine, and mirin
- Add cubed tofu
- Mix slurry with a little more water than corn starch
- Add slurry, add sesame oil
- Push around for a bit and serve
